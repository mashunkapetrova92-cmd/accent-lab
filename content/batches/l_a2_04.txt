=== a2-the-pen-pal | My Pen Pal | People | A2
When I was twelve, my English teacher gave every student in our class a pen pal. Mine was a girl called Akiko from Osaka, in Japan. We wrote letters to each other in English, because it was the only language we both knew.

In her first letter, Akiko told me about her family, her school, and her favorite food. She sent me a photo of her cat and a small origami bird. I wrote back about my town, my brother, and my love of soccer. Every letter took about two weeks to arrive, and I always ran to the mailbox after school.

We wrote to each other for six years. Our English got better and better. We told each other about our problems, our dreams, and our first loves.

Last year, I finally visited Japan. Akiko met me at the airport, and we recognized each other immediately. We cried and laughed at the same time. After twenty years, we are still friends.

=== a2-the-forgotten-umbrella | The Forgotten Umbrella | Short Fiction | A2
Every morning, Mr. Hall took the same train to work. He always sat in the same seat, next to the window, and read the newspaper. One rainy Monday, he left his black umbrella on the train.

The next morning, he saw a young woman with his umbrella. She was sitting in his seat. "Excuse me," he said, "I think that is my umbrella." The woman smiled. "I know," she said. "I found it yesterday. I wanted to give it back to you." She knew he took this train every day.

They started talking. Her name was Jane, and she worked in the library near his office. The next day, they sat together again. And the next day. Soon, they were talking all the way to the city every morning.

A year later, Mr. Hall and Jane got married. At the wedding, Mr. Hall gave a short speech. He held up the black umbrella and said, "This is the best thing I ever lost."

=== a2-plastic-in-the-ocean | Plastic in the Ocean | Environment | A2
Every year, millions of tons of plastic go into the oceans. It comes from rivers, beaches, and ships. Plastic bags, bottles, and food packages float on the water for many years, because plastic doesn't disappear easily. Some plastic stays in the sea for hundreds of years.

This is a big problem for sea animals. Turtles often eat plastic bags, because they look like jellyfish. Birds eat small pieces of plastic and feed them to their babies. Fish and whales can get stuck in old fishing nets. Many animals die every year because of plastic.

Plastic is also a problem for people. Very small pieces of plastic are now in fish, salt, and even drinking water. Scientists are still studying how this affects our health.

But we can all help. We can use fewer plastic bags and bottles. We can bring our own bags to the supermarket and carry a metal water bottle. And we can recycle more. Small changes, by millions of people, can make a big difference.

=== a2-the-grandparents-farm | Summer on the Farm | Family | A2
When I was a child, I spent every summer at my grandparents' farm. It was far from the city, in a green valley with a river. My parents worked, so I stayed there for six weeks every year.

Life on the farm was very different from my life at home. There was no television, and we went to bed early. In the morning, I woke up to the sound of the rooster. I helped my grandmother collect eggs from the chickens and my grandfather feed the cows. In the afternoon, I swam in the river with the children from the next farm.

My grandmother was a wonderful cook. Everything came from the farm: the vegetables, the milk, the eggs, and the fruit. In August, we made jam from the strawberries and plums. The whole kitchen smelled sweet.

Now my grandparents are gone, and another family lives on the farm. But I often think about those summers. They were the happiest days of my childhood.

=== a2-the-taxi-ride | The Wrong Address | Short Fiction | A2
It was late at night, and Paula was tired. She got into a taxi at the airport and gave the driver her address: "Twelve Green Street, please." The driver nodded, and they left.

After forty minutes, the taxi stopped. Paula looked out of the window. It was a small house in a street she didn't know. "This isn't my street," she said. The driver looked at his phone. "Twelve Green Road," he said. "You said Green Road." Paula laughed. "No, Green Street! It's on the other side of the city."

The driver was very embarrassed. He apologized many times. Then he turned off the meter. "I will take you home for free," he said. "It was my mistake."

On the long drive, they started talking. He told her about his family and his country. She told him about her trip. When they arrived, it was two o'clock in the morning. Paula gave him a big tip anyway. "Thank you for the interesting conversation," she said.

=== a2-the-giant-panda | The Giant Panda | Animals | A2
The giant panda is one of the most famous animals in the world. It is big, black and white, and it looks very friendly. Pandas live in the mountains of central China, in cool, wet forests.

Pandas eat almost only bamboo. An adult panda eats between twelve and thirty-eight kilos of bamboo every day. It spends about fourteen hours a day eating. Bamboo doesn't have much energy, so pandas move slowly and sleep a lot.

Baby pandas are very small when they are born. They weigh only about one hundred grams, and they are pink and have almost no fur. They stay with their mothers for about two years.

Some years ago, pandas were in danger. There were very few left, because people cut down their forests. But China created special parks to protect them, and now there are more pandas than before. They are still rare, but their future looks better.

=== a2-the-english-test | The Big Exam | School | A2
Last June, I had a very important English exam. I needed a good grade for my university application. I studied for six months. Every evening after work, I studied grammar and vocabulary for two hours.

The night before the exam, I couldn't sleep. I kept thinking about all the things I didn't know. In the morning, I couldn't eat breakfast. I arrived at the exam center an hour early.

The exam had four parts: reading, writing, listening, and speaking. The reading was OK, and the writing was not too bad. But the listening was very difficult. The people spoke very fast, and I missed some answers. I felt terrible during the lunch break.

In the afternoon, I had the speaking test. The examiner was very friendly, and I started to relax. We talked about travel and my job. I made some mistakes, but I spoke a lot. Six weeks later, I got the results. I passed with a good grade. I was so happy that I cried.

=== a2-the-bike-repair | Fixing My Bike | Daily Life | A2
Last weekend, my bike had a flat tire. Usually, I take it to the bike shop, but this time, I decided to fix it myself. I watched a video on the internet and bought a repair kit.

First, I turned the bike upside down. Then I took the wheel off. That was the easy part. Next, I had to take the tire off the wheel. It was very tight, and it took me twenty minutes. My hands were black and dirty. Then I found the hole in the inner tube. It was very small. A piece of glass made it.

I put a small patch on the hole and waited for the glue to dry. Then I put everything back together. That was harder than taking it off. Finally, I pumped up the tire.

I was very proud. The repair took two hours, and the bike shop does it in ten minutes. But I learned something new, and next time, it will be faster.

=== a2-the-bus-journey | A Long Bus Trip | Travel | A2
Last year, I traveled across South America by bus for two months. Buses are the cheapest way to travel there, and they go almost everywhere. Some trips were short, but some were more than twenty hours long.

The long-distance buses were surprisingly comfortable. The seats went back almost like a bed, and some buses served dinner. I usually traveled at night, so I didn't have to pay for a hotel. But it was not always easy to sleep, because the roads in the mountains had many curves.

From the window, I saw amazing places: high mountains with snow, green jungles, and dry deserts. I met many interesting people on the buses. An old man shared his fruit with me and told me about his village. A family taught me some words in their language.

Traveling by bus is slow, but you see the real country. I learned more on those buses than in any guidebook.

=== a2-the-singing-bird | The Bird on the Balcony | Nature | A2
Last spring, a small bird made a nest on my balcony. It was in an old flower pot in the corner. At first, I didn't notice it. Then one morning, I heard a lot of noise and saw the bird carrying small pieces of grass.

I decided not to use the balcony for a few weeks. I didn't want to scare the bird. Every morning, I watched her through the window while I drank my coffee. After about two weeks, there were four small blue eggs in the nest.

Two weeks later, the eggs opened. Four tiny babies with no feathers were in the nest. They were always hungry. The mother and father bird flew back and forth all day with insects and worms. The babies opened their mouths wide and made a lot of noise.

After three more weeks, the babies were big enough to fly. One morning, the nest was empty. I was a little sad, but the next spring, the birds came back.

=== a2-the-lost-phone | A Phone in the Taxi | Technology | A2
Last month, I left my phone in a taxi. I only noticed when I got home and wanted to call my sister. My whole life was on that phone: my photos, my contacts, my work emails, and my bank app. I felt sick.

I used my laptop to find my phone with a special app. It showed a small dot on a map. The phone was moving around the city. I called my number from my laptop, but nobody answered. I was sure that somebody had stolen it.

Then, about an hour later, my laptop rang. It was the taxi driver. He found my phone on the back seat and saw my call. "Where do you live?" he asked. "I can bring it to you after my work."

At midnight, he knocked on my door with my phone. I wanted to give him some money, but he said no. "Just do the same for somebody else one day," he said. I will never forget his kindness.

=== a2-the-best-sandwich | The Sandwich Shop | Food | A2
On the corner of my street, there is a tiny sandwich shop. It has only three tables, and there is always a line at lunchtime. The owner is a big, friendly man called Sal. He has worked there for thirty years.

Sal makes every sandwich by hand. He bakes his own bread every morning at five o'clock. He uses fresh vegetables from a farm outside the city and cheese from a small dairy. His most famous sandwich has roast chicken, cheese, tomatoes, and a special green sauce. Nobody knows the recipe for the sauce.

The sandwiches are not cheap, but they are huge. One sandwich is enough for lunch and dinner. Many office workers come from far away just to eat there.

Last year, a big company wanted to buy Sal's shop and open more shops in other cities. They offered him a lot of money, but he said no. "I don't want more money," he said. "I want to make good sandwiches for my neighbors."

=== a2-the-swimming-lesson | Learning to Swim at Forty | Health | A2
I never learned to swim as a child. My family lived far from the sea, and there was no swimming pool in our town. When I went on vacation with friends, I always stayed on the beach while they swam. I felt embarrassed.

Last year, when I turned forty, I decided it was time to learn. I found a class for adults at the pool near my office. There were five other students, and most of them were older than me. That made me feel better.

The first lesson was terrible. I was scared to put my face in the water, and I held on to the side of the pool. Our teacher, Ben, was very patient. He said, "Don't fight the water. Relax, and it will hold you." Slowly, I began to trust the water.

After three months, I could swim twenty-five meters without stopping. Last summer, I swam in the sea for the first time. I was so happy that I laughed and swallowed salty water.

=== a2-the-bakery-fire | The Night of the Fire | Short Fiction | A2
One night in winter, a fire started in the old bakery on Main Street. It was two o'clock in the morning, and everybody in the street was asleep. Only one person was awake: a teenage boy called Josh, who couldn't sleep and was looking out of his window.

Josh saw smoke coming from the bakery. He knew the baker and his family lived above the shop. He called the fire department, and then he ran outside and started knocking on doors and shouting. The neighbors woke up and came out.

The baker, his wife, and their two small children came down the stairs just in time. A few minutes later, the fire trucks arrived. The firefighters worked for two hours and stopped the fire. The bakery was badly damaged, but everybody was safe.

The next day, the whole town talked about Josh. The mayor gave him a special award. And the baker promised him free bread for the rest of his life.

=== a2-the-art-class | My Art Class | Hobbies | A2
Last autumn, I started an art class at a community center near my home. I haven't painted since I was a child, and I was nervous on the first day. But the teacher, Anna, made everybody feel welcome.

There are ten people in the class. Some are students, some are retired, and one is a doctor. We meet every Tuesday evening for two hours. In the first lessons, we drew simple things, like fruit, bottles, and flowers. Then we started to use paint. Anna showed us how to mix colors and how to paint light and shadow.

At first, my paintings were not very good. But Anna always found something positive to say. "Look at this color," she said, or "I like the shape of this apple." Every week, I learned something new.

At the end of the year, the class had a small exhibition. My painting of the river near my house was on the wall, next to the others. My family came, and my mother bought it.

=== a2-the-weekend-market | Selling at the Market | Work | A2
Every Sunday, my wife and I sell our homemade jam at a market in the town square. We started three years ago as a hobby. Now it is a small business.

We make the jam at home during the week. In summer, we use strawberries, raspberries, and cherries. In autumn, we use apples, plums, and figs. We buy the fruit from local farmers. Each batch takes about three hours, and the kitchen is always hot and sticky.

On Sunday mornings, we get up at five o'clock and load the car. At seven, we put up our small table and our tent. We always give customers a taste of the jam on a small piece of bread. Many people come back every week, and some of them are now our friends.

We don't make a lot of money, but we enjoy it very much. Last month, a small shop in the city asked to sell our jam. Maybe one day, this hobby will be our full-time job.

=== a2-the-solar-panels | Energy from the Sun | Science | A2
Last year, my parents put solar panels on the roof of their house. Solar panels use the light from the sun to make electricity. They are flat and dark blue, and they face the south, where the sun is strongest.

At first, my father was not sure. The panels were expensive, and he didn't know if they would work in our country, where it often rains. But they work even on cloudy days. They make less electricity, but they still make some.

In summer, the panels make more electricity than my parents need. The extra electricity goes to the town's power network, and the electricity company pays them for it. In winter, they make less, so my parents still buy some electricity. But overall, their electricity bill is much lower than before.

My father now talks about the panels all the time. He checks an app on his phone every evening to see how much electricity they made. He says it is better than television.

=== a2-the-opera | My First Opera | Culture | A2
Last month, my friend Sophie invited me to the opera. I have never been to an opera before, and I didn't think I would like it. I thought it was boring music for old, rich people. But Sophie had two free tickets, so I said yes.

The opera house was beautiful, with red seats, golden walls, and a huge light in the ceiling. Everybody was dressed nicely. The opera was in Italian, but there were English words on a small screen above the stage.

When the music started, I was surprised. The orchestra was amazing, and the singers had incredible voices. They sang so loudly without a microphone! The story was about two young people in love, but their families hated each other. It was very sad, and at the end, I cried a little.

The opera lasted three hours, but it didn't feel long. On the way home, I told Sophie I wanted to go again. I was wrong about opera. It isn't boring at all.

=== a2-the-clean-up | Cleaning the Beach | Environment | A2
Every year in September, our town organizes a beach clean-up day. Hundreds of volunteers come to the beach on a Saturday morning to collect trash. This year, I went for the first time with my two children.

We got big bags and gloves at the meeting point. Then we walked along the beach and picked up everything that didn't belong there. We found plastic bottles, bags, old shoes, fishing nets, and many small pieces of plastic. My son even found a broken toy car and an old phone.

After three hours, we filled six big bags. In total, the volunteers collected more than two tons of trash. The organizers told us that most of it came from the sea, not from people on the beach.

My children were shocked. "Why do people throw things in the sea?" my daughter asked. It was a good question. On the way home, we talked about how we can use less plastic at home. It was a good lesson for all of us.

=== a2-the-best-advice | The Best Advice | People | A2
When I was twenty, I was very unhappy. I didn't like my studies, I didn't have much money, and I didn't know what I wanted to do with my life. One day, I talked to my grandfather about it.

He listened quietly for a long time. Then he said something I will never forget. "You don't have to know everything now," he said. "Just take one small step. Then take another one. The path will appear when you start walking."

At the time, I didn't really understand. But I followed his advice. I left my studies and got a job in a bookstore. I loved it. After a year, I started studying literature in the evening. Then I got a job at a publishing company. Today, I edit books for a living, and I love my work.

My grandfather died five years ago. But whenever I have a difficult decision, I remember his words. One small step, then another one.
