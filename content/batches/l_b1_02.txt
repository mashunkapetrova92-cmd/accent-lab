=== b1-the-library-of-things | The Library of Things | Society | B1
How often do you use a drill? According to some estimates, the average drill is used for only about thirteen minutes in its whole life. Most of the time, it just sits in a cupboard. The same is true of many other things we own, such as ladders, tents, sewing machines, and party decorations.

This is the idea behind a new kind of library that is appearing in cities around the world: the library of things. Instead of books, these libraries lend out everyday objects. Members can borrow a vacuum cleaner, a projector, a camping stove, or a set of gardening tools for a few days, usually for a small fee.

The benefits are clear. Borrowing is much cheaper than buying, especially for things you only need once or twice a year. It also saves space at home, which is important for people who live in small apartments. And it is better for the environment, because fewer things need to be produced, transported, and eventually thrown away.

Many libraries of things also offer workshops where people can learn to repair objects or use new tools. Volunteers check that everything is clean and working before it is lent out again. Some libraries have become important meeting places, where neighbors get to know each other and share skills.

Of course, the idea depends on trust. Borrowers have to look after the things they take and return them on time. But so far, most libraries report very few problems. As one organizer said, "People are generally good. When you trust them, they usually respect that trust."

=== b1-the-everest-climb | Climbing Everest | Travel | B1
At almost eight thousand nine hundred meters, Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world. For a long time, many people believed it was impossible to climb. Then, in nineteen fifty-three, Edmund Hillary from New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa from Nepal, finally reached the top.

Since then, thousands of people have climbed Everest, but it is still extremely dangerous. At the top, the air contains only about a third of the oxygen that it does at sea level. Most climbers use bottles of oxygen to breathe. Without it, the brain and body begin to fail. Climbers can become confused, make bad decisions, and even lose consciousness.

The weather is another serious danger. Temperatures can fall below minus thirty degrees, and winds can be strong enough to knock a person off the mountain. Climbers also face avalanches and deep cracks in the ice. Every year, some climbers die on the mountain.

In recent years, Everest has also faced new problems. Climbing it has become a business, and hundreds of people now attempt it every season. On good days, long lines of climbers form near the top, waiting in the freezing cold for their turn. The mountain is also polluted with tons of trash, such as empty oxygen bottles, old tents, and food packages left behind by expeditions.

The government of Nepal has introduced new rules to protect the mountain and its visitors. Climbers now have to bring their trash back down. Still, many people believe that more needs to be done to protect the highest place on Earth.

=== b1-the-genius-of-leonardo | Leonardo da Vinci | History | B1
When people hear the name Leonardo da Vinci, most of them think of the Mona Lisa, one of the most famous paintings in the world. But Leonardo was much more than a painter. He was also a scientist, an engineer, an inventor, and a musician. Many people consider him one of the greatest minds in history.

Leonardo was born in a small town in Italy in fourteen fifty-two. As a teenager, he went to Florence to work for a famous artist, where he learned to paint, draw, and make sculptures. He quickly became known for his extraordinary talent. But he was also endlessly curious about the world around him.

Throughout his life, Leonardo filled thousands of pages of notebooks with drawings and ideas. He studied the human body by examining dead bodies, and his drawings of muscles, bones, and the heart were far more accurate than any others at the time. He watched birds for hours to understand how they flew, and he designed flying machines, a kind of parachute, and even an early version of a helicopter. He also drew plans for bridges, weapons, and machines that used water power.

Interestingly, Leonardo often wrote in his notebooks from right to left, so that his writing could only be read easily in a mirror. Nobody knows exactly why. Some think it was to keep his ideas secret, while others think it was simply easier for him, since he was left-handed.

Many of his inventions were never built in his lifetime, because the technology didn't exist yet. But his notebooks show a mind that was centuries ahead of its time.

=== b1-the-lucky-mistake | Discoveries by Accident | Science | B1
We often imagine that great scientific discoveries come from years of careful planning. But some of the most important inventions in history happened completely by accident.

One of the most famous examples is penicillin, the first antibiotic. In nineteen twenty-eight, the Scottish scientist Alexander Fleming went on vacation and left some dishes of bacteria in his laboratory. When he came back, he noticed that a mold had grown on one of them. Around the mold, the bacteria had died. Fleming realized that the mold was producing something that killed bacteria. Penicillin has since saved millions of lives.

The microwave oven was also discovered by chance. In the nineteen forties, an engineer called Percy Spencer was working with radar equipment when he noticed that a chocolate bar in his pocket had melted. He was curious, so he put some popcorn kernels near the machine, and they started to pop. A few years later, the first microwave oven was sold.

Other accidental inventions include the sticky notes that many people use in offices. A scientist was trying to create a very strong glue, but he made one that was very weak instead. For years, nobody knew what to do with it. Then a colleague realized it was perfect for making bookmarks that wouldn't fall out of his book.

However, these discoveries were not just luck. Each of these people noticed something unusual and was curious enough to investigate it. As the scientist Louis Pasteur once said, "Chance favors the prepared mind."

=== b1-the-shy-actor | The Shy Actor | Short Fiction | B1
When Oliver was a child, he was so shy that he hid behind his mother whenever visitors came to the house. At school, he never put up his hand in class, even when he knew the answer. Talking to strangers made his heart race and his face turn red.

So everyone was surprised when, at the age of fifteen, he signed up for the school drama club. Even Oliver wasn't sure why he did it. Maybe he was tired of being invisible. At the first meeting, he sat at the back and hoped nobody would notice him. But the teacher, Mrs. Clark, asked everybody to read a few lines from a play.

When it was Oliver's turn, his voice shook. But then something strange happened. As he read the words of the character, he forgot about himself. He wasn't shy Oliver anymore. He was an angry king. His voice grew louder and stronger. When he finished, the room was silent, and then everybody clapped.

Over the next few years, Oliver acted in every school play. He discovered that on stage, playing another person, he felt free in a way he never felt in real life. Offstage, he was still quiet, but he became a little more confident every year.

Today, Oliver is a professional actor who has performed in theaters all over the country. In interviews, he often says that he is still a shy person. "Acting didn't cure my shyness," he explains. "It just gave me a place where my shyness didn't matter."

=== b1-the-decline-of-bees | Why Are the Bees Disappearing? | Environment | B1
Bees may be small, but they play a huge role in our lives. When they fly from flower to flower collecting food, they carry pollen with them. This process, called pollination, allows plants to produce fruit and seeds. About a third of the food we eat, including apples, almonds, tomatoes, and coffee, depends on bees and other pollinating insects.

In recent decades, however, scientists and farmers have noticed a worrying trend. In many parts of the world, the number of bees is falling. Some beekeepers have lost a large part of their colonies in a single winter. Wild bees, which most people never notice, are also in decline.

There is not one single cause. One of the main problems is the use of pesticides, chemicals that farmers use to kill insects that damage their crops. Some pesticides harm bees as well, affecting their ability to find their way home. Another problem is the loss of habitat. As cities grow and farms get bigger, there are fewer wild flowers and fewer places for bees to live. Diseases, parasites, and climate change make the situation worse.

Fortunately, there are many things that can help. Some countries have banned the most harmful pesticides. Farmers are being encouraged to leave strips of wild flowers at the edges of their fields. And ordinary people can help too.

Planting flowers that bees like, such as lavender or sunflowers, can make a real difference, even on a small balcony. Avoiding chemicals in the garden and leaving some areas a little wild also gives bees a place to live. Every small garden can become a stop on a bee's journey.

=== b1-the-perfect-job-interview | Tips for a Job Interview | Work | B1
For many people, a job interview is one of the most stressful experiences in life. You only have a short time to show an employer that you are the right person for the job. The good news is that, with some preparation, you can feel much more confident.

The first step is to research the company. Read its website carefully, find out what it does, and look for recent news about it. Interviewers are often impressed when candidates know something about the company and can explain why they want to work there. It shows that you are genuinely interested and not just looking for any job.

Next, think about the questions you might be asked. Many interviews include common questions, such as "Tell me about yourself" or "What are your strengths and weaknesses?" Practice your answers out loud, perhaps with a friend. Prepare some examples from your experience that show your skills. For instance, if you say you are good at solving problems, describe a time when you actually solved one.

On the day of the interview, plan to arrive about ten minutes early. Dress appropriately for the type of company. Body language is important too. Smile, make eye contact, and try to sit up straight. If you don't understand a question, it is perfectly fine to ask the interviewer to repeat it.

Finally, prepare one or two questions of your own to ask at the end. You might ask about the team, the training, or the next steps in the process. And after the interview, it is a good idea to send a short email to thank the interviewer for their time.

=== b1-a-life-in-letters | Saving Handwriting | Education | B1
In many schools today, children spend more time typing on keyboards and tablets than writing by hand. Some schools have even stopped teaching joined-up handwriting altogether. After all, most adults today rarely write anything by hand except a shopping list. So is handwriting still important?

Many researchers believe it is. Several studies have shown that writing by hand helps us learn and remember better than typing. When we type, we can write very quickly, almost copying every word we hear. But when we write by hand, we are slower, so we have to think about what is important and put the ideas into our own words. This extra mental effort helps the brain to process and remember the information.

Handwriting also seems to be important for young children who are learning to read. When children form letters by hand, they activate parts of the brain that are connected to reading. Children who learn to write letters by hand often recognize them more easily than children who learn by typing.

However, others argue that keyboard skills are more useful in the modern world. Most jobs require people to type quickly and accurately, and computers make it easy to edit and share work. For children with certain learning difficulties, typing can also make writing much easier.

Perhaps the answer is that both skills are valuable. Many experts suggest that children should learn to write by hand first and then learn to type. And for adults, taking notes by hand in meetings or lectures might be an easy way to remember more.

=== b1-the-cafe-for-cats | A Cafe Full of Cats | Animals | B1
The first cat cafe opened in Taiwan in nineteen ninety-eight. The idea was simple: customers could drink coffee and eat cake while playing with cats who lived in the cafe. It quickly became popular, especially in Japan, where many people live in small apartments where pets are not allowed. Today, there are cat cafes in cities all over the world.

Visiting a cat cafe is a unique experience. Customers usually pay an entrance fee and are asked to wash their hands and follow some rules. You mustn't wake a sleeping cat, pick one up without permission, or feed them human food. The cats are free to move around as they like. Some come to sit on visitors' laps, while others ignore everybody and sleep on high shelves.

Many people visit cat cafes because spending time with animals helps them relax. Research shows that stroking a cat can reduce stress and lower blood pressure. For people who love cats but can't have one at home, it is the next best thing.

In some countries, cat cafes also help homeless cats. The cats in these cafes come from animal shelters, and visitors can adopt them if they want. This gives the cats a comfortable place to live while they wait for a new family, and it helps them to get used to people.

However, not everybody likes the idea. Some animal welfare groups worry that cats may feel stressed by constant attention from strangers. Good cat cafes take this seriously. They limit the number of visitors and give the cats quiet areas where people are not allowed.

=== b1-the-midnight-sun | The Land of the Midnight Sun | Places | B1
Imagine looking out of your window at midnight and seeing bright sunshine. For people who live in the far north of countries like Norway, Sweden, and Finland, this is a normal part of summer. For several weeks around the end of June, the sun never sets. This is known as the midnight sun.

It happens because the Earth is tilted. During the summer, the northern part of the planet is tilted toward the sun, so areas near the North Pole receive sunlight for twenty-four hours a day. In the far north of Norway, the sun doesn't set at all from the middle of May until the end of July.

For visitors, the midnight sun is a magical experience. People go hiking at two in the morning, play golf at midnight, or simply sit outside and enjoy the golden light. But it can also be difficult. Many people find it hard to sleep when it is light all night, and hotels often have special thick curtains to block out the sun.

In winter, the opposite happens. For weeks, the sun doesn't rise at all. This period is called the polar night. It isn't completely dark all day, because there is a blue light around midday, but it is very cold and dark for most of the time. Some people feel tired and sad during these months.

But winter has its own beauty. The long, dark nights are the best time to see the northern lights, when green and purple lights dance across the sky. Many people say it is one of the most beautiful sights in nature.

=== b1-the-teenage-brain | Inside the Teenage Brain | Science | B1
Parents of teenagers often complain that their children are moody, take silly risks, and can't get out of bed in the morning. For a long time, people thought this was just a normal part of growing up. But scientists now know that the teenage brain is going through a period of enormous change.

Until recently, scientists believed that the brain finished developing in childhood. Thanks to modern brain scans, we now know that it continues to develop until people are in their mid twenties. The part of the brain that changes last is at the front. This area is responsible for planning, controlling impulses, and thinking about the consequences of our actions.

Meanwhile, the parts of the brain that control emotions and rewards are very active during the teenage years. This helps to explain why teenagers often react strongly to their emotions and why they enjoy excitement and new experiences. It also explains why they care so much about what their friends think. Studies show that teenagers take more risks when they are with friends than when they are alone.

Teenagers' sleep patterns also change. Their body clock shifts, so they naturally feel sleepy later at night and want to wake up later in the morning. This is why many teenagers struggle to get up early for school. Some schools have started classes later in the morning, and they have seen improvements in students' grades and mood.

Understanding these changes can help parents and teachers be more patient. Teenage behavior is not always about being difficult. Often, it is simply a brain in the middle of a major rebuild.

=== b1-the-price-of-fast-fashion | The Cost of Cheap Clothes | Society | B1
Today, it is possible to buy a T-shirt for less than the price of a sandwich. Clothes shops update their collections every few weeks, and many people buy new clothes much more often than their parents did. This trend is known as fast fashion. It has made fashion cheaper and more accessible, but it also has a hidden cost.

Producing clothes uses enormous amounts of water and energy. Making a single pair of jeans can require thousands of liters of water, mostly to grow the cotton. Many clothes are also made from synthetic materials, such as polyester, which come from oil. When these clothes are washed, they release tiny plastic fibers that end up in rivers and oceans.

Because clothes are so cheap, people often wear them only a few times before throwing them away. In some countries, the average person throws away tens of kilos of clothing every year. Most of it ends up in landfill sites or is burned. Only a small amount is recycled into new clothes.

There are also concerns about the people who make our clothes. Many factories are in countries where workers are paid very low wages and work long hours in poor conditions. Some terrible accidents in factories have shocked the world and led to calls for change.

However, more people are now looking for alternatives. Secondhand clothes shops and online markets have become very popular, especially among young people. Some people are learning to repair their clothes, and some brands now offer to take old clothes back and recycle them. Buying fewer, better quality clothes and wearing them for longer may be the simplest solution of all.

=== b1-the-ghost-ship | The Mystery of the Mary Celeste | History | B1
In December eighteen seventy-two, the crew of a British ship saw another ship sailing strangely in the Atlantic Ocean, between Portugal and the Azores islands. It was moving slowly and seemed to have no direction. When they went closer and climbed on board, they discovered that there was nobody on it.

The ship was called the Mary Celeste. It had left New York about a month earlier, carrying a cargo of alcohol to Italy. On board were the captain, Benjamin Briggs, his wife, their two-year-old daughter, and seven sailors. But now, all of them had disappeared.

The strangest thing was that the ship was in good condition. There was plenty of food and water on board, and the passengers' personal possessions were still there. The cargo was almost complete. However, the ship's small lifeboat was missing, and some of the instruments for navigation had gone. It looked as if the crew had left in a hurry.

Nobody ever discovered what happened to the people on board, and the mystery has inspired many theories. Some people suggested that pirates had attacked the ship, but nothing valuable had been stolen. Others imagined sea monsters or giant waves. One popular theory is that alcohol from the barrels leaked and produced gas, and the captain feared an explosion. He may have ordered everybody into the lifeboat, planning to return when it was safe, but then lost contact with the ship.

The famous writer Arthur Conan Doyle wrote a story inspired by the ship, which made it even more famous. Today, the Mary Celeste remains one of the greatest mysteries of the sea.

=== b1-the-joy-of-gardening | Why Gardening Is Good for You | Health | B1
For many people, gardening is simply a hobby, a way to grow some vegetables or make the outside of their house look nice. But research shows that gardening has surprising benefits for both our physical and mental health.

First, gardening is good exercise. Digging, carrying bags of soil, pulling weeds, and pushing a lawn mower all use our muscles and raise our heart rate. An hour of gardening can burn as many calories as a gym session. And because it doesn't feel like exercise, many people do it for longer and more regularly than they would go to the gym.

Gardening is also good for the mind. Spending time outside in green spaces reduces stress and anxiety. Some scientists believe that a type of bacteria found in soil may even help to improve our mood. In several countries, doctors now sometimes recommend gardening to patients who are suffering from depression or loneliness. This is sometimes called "green prescribing."

Community gardens can be especially helpful. In these gardens, neighbors share a piece of land and grow food together. They give people the chance to meet others, share skills, and feel part of a community. For older people who live alone, they can be an important source of friendship.

Finally, growing your own food can encourage healthier eating. Children who help to grow vegetables are more likely to eat them. And there is a special pleasure in picking a tomato that you have grown yourself, still warm from the sun. You don't need a big garden to start. A few pots of herbs on a windowsill are enough.

=== b1-the-unusual-job | The Professional Sleeper | Work | B1
When people talk about dream jobs, they usually mean something exciting, like being a pilot or a famous actor. But some people have jobs that are far more unusual. One of the strangest is the job of a professional sleeper.

Some hotels employ people to test their beds. These testers spend the night in different rooms and write reports about how comfortable the beds are, whether the room is quiet, and how dark it gets at night. They might also check the pillows, the temperature of the room, and even how easy it is to find the light switch in the dark.

Mattress companies also pay people to sleep. Testers might be asked to try a new mattress for several weeks and describe how they feel when they wake up. Do they have back pain? Do they feel too hot? Their opinions help the company improve its products.

Sleep scientists need volunteers too. In sleep laboratories, researchers attach small sensors to people's heads and bodies to measure their brain activity, heart rate, and breathing while they sleep. These studies help scientists understand sleep problems and develop new treatments. Some studies last for several weeks, and participants are paid for their time.

It might sound like the easiest job in the world, but it isn't always relaxing. Testers have to pay close attention and write detailed notes, and sleeping in a strange bed with wires on your head is not easy. One former tester said that, ironically, the hardest part of the job was falling asleep.

=== b1-the-forgotten-wallet | The Honest Waiter | Short Fiction | B1
It was the end of a long, busy Friday night, and Carlos was cleaning the tables in the small Italian restaurant where he worked. He was tired, his feet hurt, and he still had to take two buses to get home. As he lifted a napkin from the last table, he found a thick black wallet.

He opened it to look for a name. Inside, there was a driver's license, several credit cards, and a large amount of cash, more money than Carlos earned in a month. For a moment, he just stood there, holding it. He thought about his rent, which was late, and the new shoes his daughter needed for school.

Then he looked at the license again. He remembered the man: an older gentleman who had eaten alone, very quietly, and had been very polite. Carlos closed the wallet and gave it to his manager. Together, they found a business card inside with a phone number and called it.

The man came back to the restaurant half an hour later. He was shaking with relief. He explained that the money was to pay for his wife's medical treatment the next morning. He tried to give Carlos some of the money as a reward, but Carlos refused. "I just did the right thing," he said.

Two weeks later, the man returned with his wife, who was now feeling much better. He told Carlos he owned a small company and was looking for an honest person to manage his office. Carlos started his new job the following month. He still visits the restaurant every Friday.

=== b1-the-history-of-the-internet | How the Internet Began | Technology | B1
Today, it is difficult to imagine life without the internet. We use it to work, study, shop, chat with friends, and watch films. But the internet is surprisingly young. Many people who are alive today remember a world without it.

The internet began in the late nineteen sixties as a project of the United States military. Researchers wanted to create a network of computers that could continue working even if part of it was destroyed. In nineteen sixty-nine, the first message was sent between two computers at universities in California. The plan was to send the word "login," but the system crashed after just two letters. So the first message ever sent on the internet was "lo."

During the nineteen seventies and eighties, the network grew slowly. It was mainly used by scientists and universities to share information. Using it was complicated, and you needed special knowledge to find anything.

The big change came in nineteen eighty-nine, when a British scientist called Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web. He created a system of pages connected by links, which anyone could read using a program called a browser. He decided not to make money from his invention, so that everybody could use it for free.

In the nineteen nineties, the web spread rapidly. Companies created websites, email became common, and people began shopping online. Then came smartphones, social media, and video streaming. Today, more than five billion people use the internet. In just a few decades, it has changed almost every part of our lives.

=== b1-the-art-of-the-nap | The Power of a Short Nap | Health | B1
In many cultures, taking a nap in the afternoon is a normal part of the day. In others, it is seen as a sign of laziness. But scientists who study sleep say that a short nap can have real benefits for our health and our work.

Most people feel a little sleepy in the early afternoon, usually between one and three o'clock. This is not only because of lunch. It is part of our natural body clock. Many experts believe that a nap at this time of day can improve concentration, memory, and mood.

The length of the nap is important. A nap of about twenty minutes is often called a "power nap." It is long enough to make you feel more alert, but short enough that you don't fall into deep sleep. If you sleep for longer, for example an hour, you might wake up feeling confused and even more tired than before. This feeling is called sleep inertia.

Some companies have started to see the benefits of napping. They provide quiet rooms or special chairs where employees can rest for a short time during the day. Some research suggests that a short nap can make workers more productive and less likely to make mistakes.

However, napping is not for everyone. People who have trouble sleeping at night are often advised to avoid naps, because they can make it harder to fall asleep later. A useful trick for a power nap is to drink a cup of coffee just before lying down. The caffeine takes about twenty minutes to work, so you wake up feeling doubly refreshed.

=== b1-the-traveling-library | The Camel Library | Education | B1
In many rural parts of Kenya, there are no libraries, and books are very expensive. Villages are often far apart, and the roads are rough or don't exist at all. For children who live there, getting access to books can be almost impossible. So the country found an unusual solution: a library carried by camels.

The camel library service began in the nineteen nineties in the northeast of the country. Each camel can carry boxes containing hundreds of books. The camels travel from village to village with a librarian and a helper, often walking for hours through hot, dry land. Camels are perfect for this job, because they can go a long time without water and can walk where cars cannot.

When the camels arrive in a village, the librarian spreads mats on the ground under a tree and lays out the books. Children and adults gather around to choose what they want to read. They can borrow books for about two weeks, until the camels return. Many villages have nobody else to bring them reading materials, so the camels' visits are a special event.

The library has had a big impact. Teachers say that children who use it read better and do better at school. It has also encouraged adults to learn to read. In some places, the camels carry solar-powered computers as well as books.

Of course, there are challenges. Sometimes the camels get sick or the weather is too hot to travel. There are also not always enough books in local languages. But for thousands of children, the camel library opens a door to a much bigger world.

=== b1-a-world-of-festivals | Unusual Festivals Around the World | Culture | B1
Every country has its own festivals, but some of them are truly unusual. Across the world, people celebrate in ways that might seem very strange to visitors.

In a small town in Spain, thousands of people gather every August for La Tomatina, the world's biggest food fight. For one hour, they throw ripe tomatoes at each other until the streets are completely red. Fire trucks then wash the streets clean. The festival started in nineteen forty-five, possibly after a fight between young people at a parade, although nobody knows the exact story.

In England, there is a festival where people chase a large round cheese down a very steep hill. The cheese can reach speeds of over a hundred kilometers an hour, so nobody actually catches it. The first person to reach the bottom of the hill wins the cheese. Every year, several people get injured, but the tradition continues.

In Thailand, the town of Lopburi holds a special festival for the monkeys that live there. People prepare huge tables of fruit, vegetables, and sweets just for the monkeys, to thank them for bringing tourists and good luck to the town. The monkeys climb all over the tables and eat everything.

In India and Nepal, people celebrate the spring festival of Holi by throwing brightly colored powder and water at each other. Streets fill with music, dancing, and clouds of pink, yellow, and green. Holi celebrates the end of winter and the victory of good over evil. On this day, social differences are forgotten, and everybody plays together.
